Credit Card Basics

The tiny piece of plastic that sparks endless debates

Let’s be honest, the words credit card can be a little triggering for a lot of people. One scroll through social media (if you dare) and you’ll see the debate play out loud and proud: some swear credit cards are dangerous, others treat them like a financial cheat code. The truth is, there isn’t a single “right” side to this conversation (there rarely is in life). What matters most is understanding what credit cards actually are and how they work. Once you have that clarity, you can make the choice that’s genuinely best for you — not the internet.

What is a Credit Card?

A credit card is a revolving line of credit, meaning it allows you to borrow money repeatedly up to a set limit. Unlike a debit card, which pulls money directly from your bank account, a credit card lets you borrow money from a lender and pay it back later.

Here’s what makes credit cards unique:

  • You borrow money temporarily
  • You must pay it back
  • Interest is added if you don't pay it back in full
  • Your usage impacts your credit score

Why a Credit Card?

Credit cards come with several benefits when used responsibly:

  • Building Credit History
    • Your card activity (credit limit, amount spent, etc..) is reported to credit bureaus, helping you establish a financial identity.
  • Fraud Protection
    • Credit cards sometimes offer stronger protection than debit cards if your card is stolen or misused.
  • Rewards and Perks
    • Many cards offer cashback, travel points, or discounts. This is one of the biggest reasons many turn towards credit cards.
  • Emergency Flexibility
    • A credit card can help cover unexpected expenses...as long as you have a plan to pay it off.

This all sounds like great stuff right! Credit cards can be a great financial tool for our everyday lives. However, it wouldn't be right for us to share the benefits of this tool without clearly explaining the negatives.

What are the Negatives About Credit Cards?

Credit cards tend to spark strong opinions because they come with several well‑known concerns that can make beginners cautious. These issues usually stem from how credit works and how quickly costs can add up if a card isn’t used responsibly. The most common concerns include:

  • High Interest Rates
    • Interest is the extra amount you pay for borrowing money, and it can add up fast if you don’t pay your balance in full.
    • Example: If you have a $500 balance with a 25% APR (Annual Percentage Rate - your interest percentage) and only make the minimum payment, interest is added every month. That $500 can easily turn into $600, $700, or more over time — not because you kept spending, but because interest kept growing on the unpaid balance.
  • Overspending Risk
    • Easy access to borrowed money can make it tempting to spend beyond your budget.
  • Credit Score Impact
    • Late payments, maxing out your limit, or opening too many accounts can lower your score and make future borrowing more expensive.

When reviewing these benefits and concerns, it's important to understand that it is possible to use credit cards responsibly, however, it simply depends on whether you feel confident managing them. If you don’t, that’s completely okay. Credit cards are not a one‑way road to financial success; they’re just a tool. Using debit cards, budgeting, and staying within your means are equally valid ways to build a healthy financial life.
